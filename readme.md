# Radar Probe Research on FMCW Radar Speed and Range Measurement Method [with MATLAB Code]

FMCW radar operates by generating FMCW signals through the transmitter, which undergo regular periodic changes in frequency with time. The majority of the signals from the FMCW radar are transmitted outward through the antenna and then return as echoes after being reflected by the target. At this point, a small portion of the signals generated by the frequency modulation transmitter is used as the local oscillator, and the echoes are mixed with the local oscillator to obtain the difference frequency. This difference frequency is amplified, converted from analog to digital (A/D), and processed to obtain the difference frequency, thereby obtaining information on the target's speed, distance, and acceleration.
The detailed steps and processes involved in signal processing for linear frequency modulation (LFM) FMCW radar have a long development history and many methods available. However, these methods share fundamental theories: firstly, the estimated target distance is based on the delay of the echo signal, and then the velocity and acceleration information are obtained through the Doppler processing step related to the echo signal. Since both the echo delay and the effective Doppler parameters are implicit in the difference frequency's frequency and phase, the problem of obtaining distance, velocity, and acceleration can be transformed into an analysis and estimation issue concerning the relevant parameters of the FMCW radar difference frequency signal.
